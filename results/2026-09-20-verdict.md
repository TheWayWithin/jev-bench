# Verdict, 20 September 2026

Jev `jev-latest` against `anthropic/claude-sonnet-4.5` through OpenRouter, 42 claims, one run
each. Raw output: `2026-09-20-full-run-scored.txt`. Per-claim results in the two JSONL files
from the same timestamp.

## The numbers

| | Jev | LLM baseline |
|---|---|---|
| Accuracy, all 42 | **85.7%** | 76.2% |
| Tier A, the real adversarial set (18) | **77.8%** | 55.6% |
| Tier B, constructed controls (24) | 91.7% | 91.7% |
| Recall on `unsupported`, the gate's job | 78.9% | 78.9% |
| Precision on `unsupported` | **88.2%** | 75.0% |
| Recall on `not_addressed` | **83.3%** | 50.0% |
| Expected calibration error | **0.093** | 0.190 |
| Latency per claim | **0.23s** | 1.68s |
| Cost per claim | **$0.000025** | $0.002000 |

Cost is measured, not estimated: OpenRouter reports what it charged, and Jev's is computed from
the token counts it returns at the published $0.042 per million input tokens with free output.

## Against the rule, which was written before the first run

- **Recall on `unsupported` at or above 0.80: FAIL, by 1.1 points.** 78.9%.
- At least the LLM's: PASS. Identical, 78.9%.
- Precision at or above 0.60: PASS. 88.2% against the LLM's 75.0%.
- Cheaper: PASS. Eighty times cheaper.
- Faster: PASS. Seven times faster.

**So Jev does not replace the gate outright, on the bar set beforehand.** It misses by one item.
It passes the second clause emphatically: keep it as a cheap pre-filter, escalate the uncertain
ones.

## The finding that matters more than the accuracy — corrected 21 September

**The LLM put all 42 of its answers in the top confidence bucket.** Mean stated confidence 0.95,
actual accuracy 76.2%. There is no bucket below 0.8 because it never went there. That is a real
calibration failure: the model is badly overconfident.

It is not the same thing as having no usable threshold, which is what this section originally
said. Tested at its own values rather than the 0.8 bucket boundary, the LLM's confidence does
separate right answers from wrong ones: restrict to its 0.98-and-above answers and coverage is
33.3% at 0.0% error; take everything at 0.95 and above and coverage is 83.3% at 14.3% error; take
everything and error rises to 23.8%. Re-derive it: `python3 risk_coverage.py --sweep-own
results/llm-20260920-114958.jsonl`.

Jev's confidence separates further down the range. Its 0.8 to 1.0 bucket, 29 of 42 items, was
right 93.1% of the time. Everything below was right 60 to 80%. That is a usable escalation
threshold: take the top bucket, send the rest to a reasoning model or a person.

The second finding is the same shape. On claims the source simply does not address, the LLM
scored 50% recall and Jev 83.3%. The LLM's failure was to pick a side. That is the predicted
weakness and it showed up exactly where it was predicted.

## Where the two sets diverge, and why that is the point

On Tier B, the constructed controls, the two are tied at 91.7%. Anyone benchmarking on easy
material would conclude these systems are equivalent. The whole gap opens on Tier A: 77.8%
against 55.6%. Tier A is real published sentences that a careful auditor marked wrong, and the
commonest kind is a true number attached to the wrong quantity.

## What Jev still missed

Four `unsupported` items called `supported`. Three of them are the same failure the article
itself made: right figures, wrong thing being measured. The spliced quotation presented as one
sentence from the paper; "sixteen points of decay" where the decay is in label accuracy, not
capability; "gains that reverse after a peak" where the cited version shows no reversal. The LLM
missed those three too, and four more besides.

So neither system is safe alone on the hardest class. Jev is better, cheaper and faster, and it
tells you when it is unsure. That combination is worth building on; a drop-in replacement for
human checking is not what either of them is.

## Honest limits

One run each, no repeats, so nothing here has an error bar. n=18 on the set that carries the
headline. The labels come from source audits produced by another model and checked by hand
against the primaries, not from an independent panel; one Tier A item is marked contested in the
data. Neither system was prompt-tuned: both saw identical state and identical wording. Latency
for the baseline is end to end through OpenRouter and includes its routing hop, so it is not
Anthropic's own latency. The baseline was given a JSON schema to answer into, after an unforced
first attempt where 14 of 18 replies spent the token budget reasoning in prose and never reached
the answer.
